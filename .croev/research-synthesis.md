## 2. Audience awareness mapping (Schwartz)

Three sub-audiences. Each one is a real cluster the existing ad library is already speaking to.

### Sub-audience A — "Crisis Cashflow Owner" (Construction / Trades / Small services)
- £250k–£800k turnover, 1–8 years trading, owner-operator
- Has a deadline (VAT bill, supplier, payroll, contract start date)
- Bank rejected or is too slow
- **Awareness: Problem-aware → Solution-aware.** Knows they need money, knows brokers exist, doesn't know which to trust.
- Served by: construction-worker VAT static, VAT-bill-on-table static, the "Barclays said no / £50k in 24h" testimonial video.

### Sub-audience B — "Defensive Operator" (Established SME, £500k+ turnover, decent credit)
- Not in crisis today. Has been declined or scared off banks before.
- Wants optionality without exposing themselves (credit pull, data spam, personal guarantees on the family home).
- **Awareness: Solution-aware → Product-aware.** Knows brokers and comparison sites exist, has been burned, is skeptical.
- Served by: the "line of credit before you need it" video, the "AI matches you to one lender" video, the anti-spam framing.

### Sub-audience C — "GGS Curious" (Owners researching gov-backed funding)
- Has heard about the Growth Guarantee Scheme or "government loans" in the news/social.
- Worried about putting the house up. Doesn't fully understand the scheme.
- **Awareness: Solution-aware on GGS, Unaware/Problem-aware on Fundsmart specifically.**
- Served by: green-injection static, the "3 billion pound government fund" video, the "government backs 70% of lender risk" video.

### Where the Quiz LP should sit
QUZ-001 should serve **A + B primarily**, with a C-flavoured hero option as a backup variant.

- **A** converts on urgency + "we won't make this worse" (no credit hit, no spam, fast). Quiz is perfect for them because the inline first step removes the "am I even eligible" anxiety in 2 clicks.
- **B** converts on optionality + dignity. Quiz gives them a private, no-impact way to test the market.
- **C** is best served by the **Advertorial** (long explainer) and a GGS-themed *hero variant* of the quiz, but not by the quiz baseline — explaining the scheme inside a quiz dilutes the mechanic.

Mapping is **Problem-aware to Solution-aware** at the top of the page, escalating to **Product-aware** by the offers screen. Sophistication level on Meta-cold UK SME funding right now is **Schwartz Level 4** — market has seen "compare 100+ lenders" and "no impact credit check" for years; we win by proprietary mechanism ("AI matches one lender, not 12") and identification ("Barclays said no even though my books were clean").

## 3. Angle families

Six angles. Each one is grounded in a primary source and tagged with awareness/sophistication levels.

### Angle 1 — Bank-Rejection Vindication ("Your books are clean. They still said no.")
- **Core message**: The bank declining you isn't a verdict on your business — it's a verdict on their post-2008 capital rules. There's a system built specifically for businesses banks won't touch.
- **Target**: Sub-audience A + B. Problem-aware → Solution-aware. Sophistication 4.
- **Pain points**:
  - "Been with Barclays for 20 years. Two weeks to tell me no. No proper reason."
  - "I'm doing 400K turnover, profitable, treated like I'm asking for a handout."
  - Losing contracts because the bank moves in weeks
  - The shame of being declined when the books are clean
- **Emotional hooks**: Vindication, righteous anger at banks, relief, professional dignity
- **Key benefits**:
  - 93% approval rate (brand-research.md, line 32)
  - Matched to one lender most likely to say yes (USP doc, paragraph 1)
  - Soft credit search only — no impact (USP doc, paragraph 1)
  - As little as 1 hour to approval (USP rule 5, compliance-safe phrasing)
- **Proof points**:
  - "After 2008, new regulations forced banks to hold more capital" (ad transcript)
  - "40% of UK small business loan applications are rejected" (ad transcript)
  - 142,468–143,953 UK businesses matched (brand-research line 32 / teardown line 60)
  - James M. Manchester — £75k in 24h (teardown case study)
- **Compliance notes**: Do NOT compare to Bounceback Loan. Do NOT say "instant decision" — use "as little as 1 hour". OK to cite the 40% rejection stat because it's a public BBB-adjacent figure that Fundsmart already uses in production ads.
- **Best for**: Quiz hero (Sub A/B), Sales LP, Advertorial

### Angle 2 — Anti-Spam / AI-Matched ("One lender. One decision. No comparison-site cowboys.")
- **Core message**: Comparison sites sell your data to a dozen brokers who hard-pull your credit. We use AI to pick one matched lender and stop there.
- **Target**: Sub B. Solution-aware → Product-aware. Sophistication 4–5 (mechanism + identification).
- **Pain points**:
  - "Within an hour my phone was ringing off the hook. Brokers asking for the same documents over and over."
  - "Three of them submitted my details to lenders without even asking me. It tanked my credit score."
  - "Felt like I'd been mugged off."
  - Wasted time providing the same docs five times
- **Emotional hooks**: Control, dignity, contempt for cowboys, relief
- **Key benefits**:
  - Single best-match lender, not a shotgun (USP, brand-research line 41)
  - Data never sold (USP, brand-research line 41)
  - Soft credit search only — no impact
  - Human concierge if first match doesn't approve (USP paragraph 3)
- **Proof points**:
  - 93% approval rate (when only one lender is asked)
  - "Matched to one lender" — explicit on /landinggov ("the lender most likely to approve, not the one paying us the most")
  - FCA-regulated partners: Iwoca, Funding Circle, Halo, Funding Options by Tide, Bizcap, Lending Box (teardown line 62)
- **Compliance notes**: Safe angle. Do NOT use "pre-approved" anywhere — even the ad transcript's "they've pre-approved you" line must be rewritten as "they pre-qualified you" (USP rule 1). Do NOT say "risk-free." OK to say "control & flexibility" (USP rule 8).
- **Best for**: Quiz, Sales LP, Advertorial

### Angle 3 — GGS / Government-Backed ("Your house stays safe. The government backs 70%.")
- **Core message**: The Growth Guarantee Scheme exists because banks won't lend to you. The government backs 70% of the lender's risk — that's how a "no" becomes a "yes" at decent rates, with your home off the table.
- **Target**: Sub C primarily, Sub A secondarily. Solution-aware on GGS, Product-unaware on Fundsmart. Sophistication 3 (mechanism-led: explain the 70%).
- **Pain points**:
  - "Won't risk the house"
  - Held back from growth because every bank wants a charge on the home
  - Confused by what GGS actually is
- **Emotional hooks**: Safety of family, legacy, ambition unblocked, patriotism (UK govt)
- **Key benefits**:
  - Borrow £1k–£250k under GGS (ad transcript)
  - Your home stays safe (USP rule 3 — compliant phrasing in gov context)
  - Better rates than you'd qualify for alone, because government backs 70% of lender risk
  - Soft credit search only
- **Proof points**:
  - "UK government created a nearly £3 billion fund" (ad transcript)
  - "Government backs 70% of the lender's risk" (ad transcript)

- **Compliance notes**:
  - **NEVER** say "no personal guarantee" in a GGS ad. Use only "your home stays safe" (USP rule 3).
  - **NEVER** compare GGS to Bounceback Loan (USP rule 7 — FCA / British Business Bank flag).

### Angle 4 — Line-of-Credit-Before-You-Need-It ("Win the next £50k contract instead of turning it down.")

## 4. Quiz-specific recommendations (QUZ-001)

### 4a. Recommended angles

- **Primary**: **Angle 2 — Anti-Spam / AI-Matched.** Strongest on cold Meta traffic. Sub B is the biggest addressable segment, the pain ("brokers spammed me, tanked my credit") is universal and *not* served by the /landinggov control's hero. The mechanism ("one matched lender, no spam") is the proprietary wedge Schwartz Level 4 demands.
- **Backup 1**: **Angle 1 — Bank-Rejection Vindication.** Highest emotional pull, broadest audience, already proven by the Barclays testimonial ad. Use this if Anti-Spam underperforms on first-week tests.
- **Backup 2**: **Angle 5 — VAT/HMRC Crisis Bridge.** Tightest match to high-intent traffic; works best on retargeting and on VAT-due timing (Jan, Apr, Jul, Oct). Use for a seasonal split.

### 4b. Recommended quiz mechanic (5–7 questions)

The /landinggov funnel uses a 2-inline + 4-modal structure. Our quiz should *out-trust* it by adding a scored output and reordering to put pre-qualification signals *earlier*. Proposed questions:

1. **Q1 (inline)**: "How much funding do you need?" — keep the £10k–£1m+ chip grid from the control. Familiar, fast.
2. **Q2 (inline)**: "What's it for?" — Growth / Working Capital / VAT or HMRC / Equipment / Bridge a contract. **Add "VAT or HMRC" — control is missing this and it's a top-3 funding reason per the static ads.**
3. **Q3 (inline)**: "When do you need the money?" — Today / This week / This month / Just exploring options. Surfaces urgency for routing.
4. **Q4 (modal opens)**: "What's your annual turnover?" — chip ranges: <£200k / £200k–£500k / £500k–£1m / £1m–£3m / £3m+. **Chip ranges instead of free-text** to disqualify <£200k early (the eligibility floor from brand-research line 14) without making them type.
5. **Q5**: "How long have you been trading?" — <1yr / 1–3yr / 3–5yr / 5yr+. <1yr off-ramps politely (eligibility floor from brand-research line 15).
6. **Q6**: Companies House search (their best magic trick — but pulled forward from step 1-of-modal to here, with reduced friction because the user has *already qualified themselves* on Q4/Q5 and feels invested).

Then a **Funding Fitness Score** screen (0–100, gamified) **before** the contact ask. This is the upgrade vs. /landinggov: rather than a binary "Good news — your business meets criteria" message, we show a real score, the three strongest reasons we got there, and the indicative funding range — *then* ask for email and phone to "send the full report and unlock your matched lender."

### 4c. Recommended hook for Q1

Start with the amount picker. It is familiar, it keeps the form moving, and it avoids introducing subjective segmentation before the user has asked for lender matching.

### 4d. Where Fundsmart's funnel is weakest and how we exploit it

Cribbed from teardown section "Friction Points / Weaknesses" — the four most exploitable on the quiz:

1. **No financial qualification in the funnel** (teardown line 320). They let £50k-turnover businesses fill out the whole form and then reject them at offers. We pre-qualify on Q4/Q5 and route them out with a "we're not the right fit, here's who is" page — turning a wasted lead into goodwill (and possibly a referral asset).
2. **Companies House magic is hidden until step 3** (teardown hypothesis 1). We move it to Q7 *after* identity + financial qualification, but we still **promise** the "we'll pull your business automatically from Companies House" magic on the **hero** of the LP. Pre-loaded credibility.
3. **"FINAL STEP" is a lie** (teardown line 244, weakness 8). We use an honest continuous % progress bar (teardown hypothesis 3) and bet on credibility lift — Sub B Defensive Operators are *exactly* the segment that punishes obvious gimmicks.
4. **Hero H1 animates between three states**, two of which are functionally redundant (teardown weakness 11). We use a single static H1 that locks in the Anti-Spam mechanism in one read.

## 5. Open questions for Max (the client)

These would tighten copy materially. Listed by impact.

1. **Lender count**: brand-research says "100+ FCA-regulated lenders compete" and the existing ad says "matches you across 250 lenders." Which is the legally defensible number we can put on the page?
2. **Real funded average time-to-fund**, not the fastest-case. We have "as little as 4 hours" (USP rule 6) and a 48h headline — what's the *median* so we can use it honestly in the body copy without burning the headline?
3. **Offers screen content** (teardown line 271). We didn't capture it. Whatever's on it should set the conviction ceiling for the quiz's "Funding Fitness Score" result page.
4. **What the *typical declined* applicant looks like** — Fundsmart's 93% approval is the lender's number after we filter; what's the floor on the page that we should hint at to keep expectations honest?
5. **Sole trader off-ramp**: USP rule 4 says no sole-trader lending. The control has a "I'm a Sole Trader →" branch — what does it route to? (If it's a dead end, our Q5 trading-time gate handles it; if it routes to a partner, we should match that.)
6. **Asset finance / invoice finance volume**: USP rule 2 lets us claim "no PG" but only judiciously. What % of Fundsmart's funded book is AF/IF? Determines how prominent we can make the "no PG on some products" hook.
7. **GGS authority**: are we allowed to use the British Business Bank logo / "Growth Guarantee Scheme" trademark on the page, or do we have to refer obliquely to "government-backed funding"? (Compliance gate; copy depends on it.)
8. **Funnel events 503s** (teardown line 305). Cosmetic for our copy, but if their step-tracking is broken we can't measure step-level lift from the Quiz vs. /landinggov. Worth flagging now.
9. **Meta pixel firing on step events** (teardown line 307). If pixels aren't firing on step events, the ad account can't optimize on completion. This affects our ability to attribute lift to specific angles in the 30-day testing sprint.
10. **Verbatim VOC from real applicants**: do they have a closed-won survey, post-funding NPS, or even support-call transcripts? The ad transcripts are our cleanest VOC source today; one batch of real customer language would 2x the copy precision.
